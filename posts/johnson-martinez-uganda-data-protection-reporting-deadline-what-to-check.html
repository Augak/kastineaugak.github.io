Uganda’s Data Protection Reporting Deadline: What Organisations Should Check Now

Uganda’s PDPO reminded registered organisations to submit annual compliance reports by 23 November 2026. Here are practical next steps for organisations reviewing their obligations.

Uganda · Cybersecurity · Privacy · Data Protection · Digital Policy

A deadline is a prompt to check the whole process

Uganda’s Personal Data Protection Office (PDPO) reminded registered data controllers, data collectors and data processors to prepare and submit annual Data Protection and Privacy Compliance Reports by Monday, 23 November 2026. That date has now passed. Organisations that were within the notice’s scope should check whether they submitted the required report and retain evidence of submission.

Start by confirming your status

Do not assume that every organisation has identical reporting duties. Review the PDPO notice and the Data Protection and Privacy Act, 2019, and confirm the organisation’s registration status, role in processing personal data and any instructions that apply to its circumstances. Where the position is unclear, seek guidance from the PDPO or qualified legal counsel rather than relying on a generic checklist.

Build an accurate record of data processing

A practical review should identify what personal data the organisation collects, why it collects it, where it is stored, who can access it, which service providers process it and how long it is retained. Include websites, mobile apps, staff records, customer databases, CCTV, marketing lists and third-party tools. Data collected for one purpose should not quietly become a general-purpose asset.

Check the controls behind the policy

Privacy statements matter, but they are not a substitute for operational controls. Review access permissions, authentication, backups, encryption where appropriate, staff training, incident reporting, vendor agreements and deletion procedures. Test whether a staff member can access records they do not need for their job. Check whether former staff accounts have been disabled and whether exported spreadsheets are stored safely.

Make accountability visible

Assign a person or team to coordinate privacy work. Keep a record of decisions, training, incidents, complaints and corrective actions. If a service provider handles personal data, understand its responsibilities and the safeguards in the contract. A policy that nobody owns is unlikely to be followed consistently.

What if the organisation missed the deadline?

The PDPO’s notice is the primary source for the reporting requirement and submission process. If an organisation believes it was required to report but missed the deadline, it should contact the Office promptly, explain the position accurately and ask what steps are appropriate. Do not backdate documents or claim that a report was submitted when it was not.

Privacy is a trust issue as well as a compliance issue

People share information with schools, employers, financial services, clinics and online platforms because they expect it to be handled responsibly. A well-run privacy programme reduces avoidable exposure and helps an organisation explain how it protects that trust. This article is general information, not legal advice; organisations should consult the PDPO and qualified counsel for their specific obligations.
