What can you write about?

Bring us useful ideas, practical experience and fresh perspectives. Topics may include:

  • Technology, AI tools and digital skills that make everyday work easier.
  • Ugandan and East African innovation, businesses and community solutions.
  • Research explained in clear language, with links to the original work.
  • Software development, cybersecurity, education and digital public services.
  • Creative work, media, music technology and lessons from building projects.
  • Honest product experiences, tutorials and thoughtful opinion pieces.

What contributors receive

  • Your name and a short biography alongside accepted work.
  • Links to your professional profile, portfolio or research profile where relevant.
  • A public article page that can be shared, discussed and saved by readers.
  • Inclusion in the publication archive and consideration for newsletter features.
  • Respectful editorial feedback when a piece needs clarification or improvement.

We want your work to be discoverable and useful. Clear writing, original thinking and trustworthy sources help readers find value in what you share.

How your article reaches readers

  1. Send your idea. Use the submission form to share your article, a short summary and any useful source links.
  2. Editorial review. An editor checks clarity, originality, accuracy, attribution and reader value. We may contact you with a question or suggested improvement.
  3. Publication. Approved articles are published with your byline and become available to readers. Submitting does not automatically publish an article.
  4. Join the conversation. Readers can react and comment on published work. You can share the public article link with your community.

You keep ownership of your writing. By submitting, you confirm that the work is yours or that you have permission to share it, and you allow the publication to host and promote an accepted article with credit.

A few things that make an article stronger

Start with the question or problem you want to address. Explain why it matters, use examples people can follow, link to original sources when making factual claims, and make it clear when you are sharing personal experience or opinion. Credit photographs, charts and other material that is not yours.

There is no need to sound complicated. Write for a curious reader, explain unfamiliar terms and make every section earn its place.

KEEP CREATING
